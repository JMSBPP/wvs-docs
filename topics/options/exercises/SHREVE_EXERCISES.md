## Statement

Consider a bank long $\text{Call} \, (\Omega; K)$ at $t_0$

$$
\begin{aligned}
    P^{\text{Call}\Big (\Omega ;K \Big)}_{t_0}  &=
    \frac{1}{1+r_f} \bigg[ \left( \frac{(1+r_f) - 1/u}{u - 1/u} \right) \left( u\, P_{(X, t_0)} - K \right)^{+} 
    + \, \left( \frac{u - (1+r_f)}{u - 1/u} \right) \left( \frac{1}{u}\, P_{(X, t_0)} - K \right)^{+} \bigg]\end{aligned}
$$	
	

with $\frac{1}{u}  \, <\, (K/P_{(X, t_0)} )\, < \, u $;  and the bank wants to earn $r_f$ (the risk-free no-arbitrage net rate whose gross factor satisfies $\frac{1}{u} < 1+r_f < u$); regardless of $\Omega = \{1/u, u\}$. This is, the bank is demanding the payoff:

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\Pi \, \equiv \, \Pi^{\star} \, \underbrace{- \Pi^{\text{Call}\, (\Omega, K)}}_{\text{after collecting the payoff of the option}}\, &= \, (1\ +\, r_f) \, P^{\text{Call}\Big (\Omega ;K \Big)}_{t_0} \, - \, \max \, \Big ( P_{(X, 0)} \,- \, K, 0 \Big)
\end{aligned}
$$

without investing any more money than the one already committed, $P^{\text{Call}\Big (\Omega ;K \Big)}_{t_0}$. How should the trader invest in the stock and money markets to achieve $\Pi$?

#### **Solution:**

Express every payoff as a **return profile** (divide by $P_X$). Rows index the states
$\omega \in \Omega = \{u, 1/u\}$; columns index the traded assets. The call's payoff, with the
positive-part applied (so the out-of-the-money down state is $0$ under $K/P_X > 1/u$), is the state
column



$$
\begin{aligned}
	\pi_{C(K;X)} \, &= \,
	\begin{bmatrix}
	u - K/P_X \\
	0
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The bank wants to earn the gross risk-free factor $(1+r_f)$ on its committed capital regardless of
the state:

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\overline{\pi}^{\text{bank}} \, &= \, 
	(1+r_f)\begin{bmatrix}
	   1 \\
	   1
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The premium it has committed, in return units, is priced by the risk-neutral weights:

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\frac{P_{C(K; X)}}{P_{X}} \, &= \,
	\frac{1}{1+r_f} \, \begin{bmatrix}
	\frac{(1+r_f) - 1/u}{u - 1/u}, & \frac{u - (1+r_f)}{u - 1/u}
	\end{bmatrix} \, \pi_{C(K;X)}
\end{aligned}
$$

The trader builds a portfolio $\Delta = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta_X \\ \Delta_B \end{bmatrix}$ whose
per-$P_X$ payoff matrix (stock return $u, 1/u$; money-market gross return $1+r_f$) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\Pi_{(X, B)} \, &= \,
	\begin{bmatrix}
		u & 1+r_f \\
		1/u & 1+r_f
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The market is complete and square ($u \neq 1/u$ so $\Pi_{(X,B)}$ is invertible), so the **single
replication equality** uniquely determines $\Delta$ — no separate budget constraint is imposed; the
budget is recovered below as a theorem. Being **long** the call, its payoff adds to the bank's
wealth, so the trading portfolio must supply riskless growth *minus* the call:

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		u & 1+r_f \\
		1/u & 1+r_f
	\end{bmatrix}
	\, \begin{bmatrix} \Delta_X \\ \Delta_B / P_X \end{bmatrix}\, &= \, 
	(1+r_f)\,\frac{P_{C(K; X)}}{P_{X}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
	\, - \, 
	\begin{bmatrix}
		u - K/P_X \\
		0
	\end{bmatrix}; \\
	\\
	\frac{1}{u}  \, <\, (K/P_{X} )\, < \, u ; \\
	\\
	\frac{1}{u} < 1+r_f < u
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving the $2\times2$ system (verified via sympy `linsolve`):

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\Delta_X^{\star} \, &= \, -\,\frac{u - K/P_X}{u - 1/u} \, < \, 0
	\qquad(\text{short the stock}), \\
	\\
	\frac{\Delta_B^{\star}}{P_X} \, &= \, +\,\frac{u - K/P_X}{u - 1/u} \, = \, -\,\Delta_X^{\star}
	\qquad(\text{lend in the money market}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The two legs are exact negatives, so the trading portfolio's dollar cost vanishes — it is
**self-financing**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\begin{bmatrix} P_X & 1 \end{bmatrix}\,\Delta^{\star} \, &= \, P_X\Big(\Delta_X^{\star} + \tfrac{\Delta_B^{\star}}{P_X}\Big) \, = \, 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

No capital is committed beyond the premium $P_{C(K;X)}$, which by construction grows to
$(1+r_f)\,P_{C(K;X)}$ in **both** states. The long call is neutralized by shorting
$\frac{u-K/P_X}{u-1/u}$ units of stock and lending the proceeds, leaving a riskless position.

## Economic Meaning

The scalar $\bar\phi^\star$ is the **constant additive base-fee floor of an Algebra-style dynamic-fee CFMM pool** — the on-chain `baseFee` parameter that sits *underneath* the volatility-adaptive logistic surcharge $G(\tau)$ (the sigmoid adaptive fee $\phi_{Z}(\,;\sigma)$ of [FINANCE.md](../../cfmm-discrete/FINANCE.md)). It is the one degree of freedom the pool designer fixes at $t_0$; the surcharge $G(\tau)$ then rides on top of it, modulating the per-swap take with realized volatility $\sigma_X$.

Concretely, $\bar\phi^\star$ is chosen so that the LP's *accumulated cashflow net of impermanent loss* exactly funds the target lookback-call liability:

$$
CF_T(\bar\phi^\star) \;=\; \Upsilon_T - IL_T \;=\; \Pi^{\mathrm{target}}(T),
$$

where $\Upsilon_T$ is cumulative fee revenue (the base-fee $\bar\phi^\star$ applied to the volume $\Delta_X(\tau)$ weighted by the in-range liquidity measure $\langle L_K, \mu_\tau^\phi\rangle$), $IL_T$ is the impermanent loss the LP carries from rebalancing across ticks, and $\Pi^{\mathrm{target}}(T)$ is the exogenous payoff target (DEP-03 EXOGENITY hypothesis $\partial\Pi^{\mathrm{target}}/\partial\Theta = 0$). Solving the affine identity $CF_T(\bar\phi) = \Pi^{\mathrm{target}}(T)$ for $\bar\phi$ gives the closed form above: the base-fee level at which fee accrual minus impermanent loss precisely replicates the demanded liability. Economically this is the **break-even base-fee that turns the LP position into a self-funding hedge of the lookback call** — fee income offsets both the IL drag and the option payoff owed.

**Well-definedness proviso.** The closed form requires the denominator

$$
\sum_{\tau=t_0}^{T} \Delta_X(\tau)\,\langle L_K, \mu_\tau^\phi\rangle \;\neq\; 0,
$$

i.e. the cumulative volume-weighted in-range liquidity is non-degenerate over $[t_0, T]$. This is exactly the DEP-02 guard $A_t \neq \emptyset$ (the feasible-swap support of $\mu_\tau^\phi$ is non-empty so the measure integrates to a positive mass): if no trade ever crosses the LP's active range, there is no fee base to solve against and $\bar\phi^\star$ is undefined.
