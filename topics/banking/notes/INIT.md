## NUMBER OF STATES != NUMBER OF SECURITIES -> INCOMPLETE FINANCIAL MARKETS
- $\underbrace{S=I}_{\text{both, investors and borrwoers would be able to diversify and share risk optimally}}$

- $S \neq I$ *perfect diversification*  does not lie on the feasible set and **FI** is needed
- Transform financial contracts/securities **given** incomplete financial markets with the objective of servicing optimal risk sharing among risk market pariticipants while minizing transaction cost $C_A$



One of the dimensions of liquidity is subject to the number of assets and include the unit of account (cash). Given $A_N \, = \{a_1, \cdots a_N \,\}$ agents on the $L(\Omega)$ market. The optimal deposit and lending rates are given by


\[
\begin{aligned}
r_D^{\star}
&\overset{D}{=}
\frac{\Delta_X}{L_K}
\\[1em]
r_L^{\star}
&\overset{D}{=}
\left(
\frac{\bar{\sigma}_X(\cdot)}
     {2\,\phi(\bar{\sigma}_X(\cdot);\cdot)}
\right)^2
\end{aligned}
\]

Denoting $BL$ for the borrowed liquidity positions:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pi^{FI}
&\overset{D}{=}
r_L\,BL
-
r_D\,L
-
C(L,BL)
\end{aligned}
\]

The financial intermediary funding balance is defined as:


\[
\begin{aligned}
E^{FI} (t; \cdot)\, \overset{D}{=} \, FB^{FI}(t;\cdot)
&\overset{D}{=}
P_{\pi_X}(t;\cdot)
-
CF(t;\cdot)
\end{aligned}
\]



The gross-base liquidity is defined as, and financed by

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_0
&\overset{D}{=}
L_0
\\
&
\overset{F}{=}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
L^{(i)}
\end{aligned}
\]

And the base borrowed liquidity stock is defined as:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_0
&\overset{D}{=}
\sum_{j=1}^{M}
BL_K^{(j)}
\end{aligned}
\]



From where the leverage multiplier is defined as:

\[
\begin{aligned}
m (t)
&\overset{D}{=}
\frac{M^{FI} (t)}{M_0} \, = \, \frac{L_t + B_t}{M_0} \\
\\
\implies \\
M^{FI} (t) \, \overset{D}{=} \, L(t) - B (t)
\end{aligned}
\]

which is the gross-intermediary balance-sheet

From where the net inter-protocol funding position isbalance-sheet flow:

$$
\begin{aligned}
	N^{FI} (t) \, &\overset{D}{=} \, (1 - m (t)) \, L(t) - B (t)
\end{aligned}
$$

and then gross FI balance-sheet expansion/contraction is defined as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\Delta M (t) \, &= \, \Delta L(t) + \Delta B(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

 We define the region:

\[
\Delta M(t)
\overset{F}{=}
FB^{FI}(t)
\]

meaning that growth (or contraction) of the intermediary balance sheet is financed by the funded balance.



\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta M^{FI}(t) > 0
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
FB^{FI}(t) > 0,
\qquad
\text{Funding Accumulation Region}
\\[1em]
\Delta M^{FI}(t) < 0
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
FB^{FI}(t) < 0,
\qquad
\text{Funding Depletion Region}
\end{aligned}
\]

and the net inter-protocol funding flow:

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\Delta N^{FI} (t) \, &= \, \underbrace{(1- m(t-1))\Delta L(t)}_{\text{deposit funding effect}} \underbrace{- \,L(t) \, \Delta m (t) }_{\text{leverage intensity effect}} \underbrace{-\, \Delta B(t)}_{\text{        credit demand effect}}
\end{aligned}
$$


\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta N^{FI}(t) > 0
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(1-m(t-1))\Delta L(t)
>
L(t)\Delta m(t)
+
\Delta B(t)
\\
&\qquad
\text{Funding Relief Region}
\\
&\qquad
\text{Deleveraging Region}
\\
&\qquad
\text{Credit Absorption Region}
\end{aligned}
\]


\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pi^{FI}
&\overset{D}{=}
r_L\,B -
r_D\,L
+
r_M \, N^{FI}
-
C(L,B)
\end{aligned}
\]





We introduce the securitization flow as the reaction for credit expansion through creation of new contingent claims:


\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \mathcal{S}_t :
\Delta B(t)
\longrightarrow
\Pi(\Omega).
\end{aligned}
\]
